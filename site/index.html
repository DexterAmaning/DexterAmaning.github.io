How I work

Standards across these projects

Accuracy on a single split is easy to report and easy to get wrong. These are the checks the projects apply instead, wherever they fit the question.

  • Leakage-free evaluation

    Imputation, scaling, encoding and threshold selection are fitted inside cross-validation folds, never on the full dataset.

  • Calibration and operating points

    Predicted risks are checked against observed rates, and thresholds are set for a stated sensitivity target rather than for accuracy.

  • Limitations beside results

    Each project names its population, its missing external validation and what it must not be used for.

  • Honest negative results

    When a method doesn't beat a simple baseline, as Prophet didn't for Medicaid spending, the project says so.

  • Reproducible by default

    Public data with provenance and checksums, executed notebooks, pinned requirements and automated tests.

  • Documented self-correction

    Each public repository records what was wrong in its earlier version, such as an inverted target or placeholder data, and how it was fixed.

About

A clinical lens on healthcare data

I come to data science from dentistry and public health. That background shapes how I read a dataset: what a variable means in the clinic, which values are physiologically impossible, and when a “prediction” is really a restatement of how the label was defined.

The projects on this site apply that lens to public datasets in cardiology, diabetes, autism screening and pharmaceutical spending. NKABOM extends it to oral-systemic health as a synthetic-data research prototype.

Research interests

  • Oral-systemic health and the dental visit as a point of contact for health awareness (NKABOM)
  • Evaluation and calibration of clinical prediction models (heart, diabetes)
  • Validity of screening instruments and their labels (AQ-10)
  • Pharmaceutical spending and health economics (Medicaid)
  • Responsible, reproducible healthcare data science (all projects)
