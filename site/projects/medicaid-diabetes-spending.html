Medicaid spending on diabetes medicines, 2020–2024

Gross Medicaid spending on diabetes medicines rose from $7.78bn to $13.70bn. GLP-1 receptor agonists went from 19% to 53% of that spending, and a backtest showed that Prophet adds nothing over a simple drift baseline.

Data
CMS Medicaid Spending by Drug (data year 2024 release), national totals
Scope
Gross, pre-rebate spending, 2020–2024
Method
Drug-class grouping, growth decomposition, backtested forecasts
Status
Descriptive analysis; projections are scenarios, not forecasts

The question

Diabetes treatment has shifted towards newer branded medicines. How did that shift show up in Medicaid's spending, which drug classes drove it, did growth come from more prescriptions or costlier ones, and can a forecasting model predict where spending goes next?

Data

  • Source: the official CMS Medicaid Spending by Drug file (data year 2024 release). It is a U.S. government work, committed to the repository and verified against a SHA-256 checksum.
  • Rows used: national Overall rows only.
  • Scope decision: obesity-labelled incretins (Wegovy, Zepbound, Saxenda) are reported separately and excluded from diabetes totals.

Approach

  1. Verify the raw file against its checksum and keep the national rows.
  2. Classify products into therapeutic classes from the generic name, with unit tests for the classification rules.
  3. Reshape the year columns to long format and aggregate by class and year.
  4. Decompose log spending growth exactly into a claim-volume part and a spending-per-claim part.
  5. Backtest naive, drift, linear-trend and Prophet models: fit on 2020–2022, score on held-out 2023–2024.

  • GLP-1 receptor agonists rose from 19% to 53% of diabetes-medicine spending. Most of the class's growth came from more claims rather than higher spending per claim.
  • Diabetes spending peaked in 2023 ($14.82bn) and fell in 2024. The cause was insulin: gross spending fell 57% while claims fell 10.5%, consistent with the 2024 insulin list-price changes.
  • Across all classes, most growth came from higher spending per claim. That reflects a shift towards branded incretins, not price increases on the same products.

Forecasting: an honest negative result

Each model was fitted on 2020–2022 and scored on the held-out 2023–2024 values.

Backtest: fit 2020–2022, score 2023–2024
ModelMean MAPE across 5 seriesMAPE, all-diabetes total
Drift24.3%10.9%
Linear trend25.5%10.9%
Prophet (linear, no seasonality)25.6%10.9%
Naive (last value)34.9%17.8%

With annual data Prophet has no seasonality to model, and three training points can't support any changepoints, so it reduces to a straight line and adds nothing over simple baselines. No model anticipated the 2024 fall in insulin spending. The notebook's 2025–2026 numbers are therefore presented as illustrative trend scenarios, not forecasts.

Why no uncertainty intervals are shown. In the same backtest, Prophet's 80% interval contained 0 of the 10 held-out values (5 series × 2 years). Its interval reflects only the scatter of three points around a line, not real forecast error, and five annual observations are too few to validate an interval from any method.

Limitations and appropriate use

  • Gross, pre-rebate spending. Medicaid rebates are large for brand-name drugs; net cost and its trend aren't observable here.
  • No indication data. SGLT2 inhibitors are also used for heart failure and kidney disease.
  • Five annual points. Too few to validate a forecasting model or a prediction interval. The backtest has only two held-out years per series, and its 10 held-out values aren't independent.
  • National aggregates only, with no link to clinical outcomes.

What changed from the earlier version

The earlier notebook loaded the CMS file but ran its trend analysis and Prophet forecasts on hand-typed placeholder numbers, and its README described SARIMAX and regression models that weren't implemented. It was replaced entirely; every reported figure is now computed from the CMS data.

Reproduce

.venv\Scripts\activate        # macOS/Linux: source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/download_data.py   # verifies the committed CMS file
pytest
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace notebooks/medicaid_diabetes_spending.ipynb
